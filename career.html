My Career in Brief

I’m a product and organizational leader who helps teams navigate complexity with clarity, calm, and a shared sense of purpose. People often describe me as a leader who listens deeply, communicates the “why” behind decisions, and makes even the most ambiguous problems feel solvable.

I’m drawn to challenges with many moving parts—the kinds that require multiple disciplines, systems, and perspectives to work together toward something bigger than any one team could achieve alone. I thrive in environments where the work genuinely matters: products that make life easier, spark creativity, or help people connect in more meaningful ways.

Across startups, high-growth companies, and enterprise-scale organizations, the through-line in my career has been the same: find smart, kind people; learn relentlessly; and pursue the work that promises the most growth—for myself, the team, and the business. That's where the most meaningful impact happens.

How I Work

A few themes show up again and again in how I lead product and build organizations:

Clarity in Complexity

When things feel messy or morale is slipping, I start by asking questions—not to interrogate, but to reveal where understanding has diverged. Most “alignment issues” are really clarity issues. I help teams name the real problem, make the options explicit, and ensure everyone—from leadership to the front line—understands what we’re doing and why. Once that clarity exists, everything else becomes easier: execution, communication, and confidence.

Building High-Trust Teams

Trust begins with leaders who tell the truth and respect their teams’ intelligence. I believe in giving people the full context behind decisions and being transparent when I don’t have all the answers. Teams move faster and collaborate more effectively when they’re not guessing at hidden agendas or trying to interpret half-information. My style is open, direct, and grounded in the idea that honesty is the simplest and most powerful leadership tool.

Systems & Operating Rhythms

Healthy organizations need structure, but not bureaucracy. I design lightweight rhythms that keep teams aligned without slowing them down—from weekly all-hands syncs that share executive-level context, to clear quarterly goals, to rituals like demos, one-pagers, story maps, and pre-launch alignment. The goal isn’t to impose process; it’s to create shared understanding so teams can operate with autonomy and avoid the chaos that drains creativity and energy.

Thoughtful Product Strategy

Good strategy comes from staying close to customers and connecting their needs to the realities of the market, the business, and the technology. I spend time in the field, work alongside sales, join customer conversations, and partner with support and success teams to understand both delight and pain points. With that foundation, I help teams make clear bets, see around corners, and focus on the work that creates meaningful impact rather than noise or novelty.

My Career Journey

Foundations

Learning How Great Products Really Get Made

Early in my career, I realized that great products don’t come from a single discipline—they emerge when engineering, design, research, go-to-market, operations, sales, and support understand one another’s worlds. I spent those early years shadowing colleagues across functions, learning how each piece of the system worked and what it took for teams to operate well together.

I also discovered that I had a natural instinct for resolving ambiguity. People from outside my immediate team began coming to me for help clarifying problems, framing decisions, and corralling stakeholders. That pattern—helping teams find clarity so they could move forward—became the earliest signal that product leadership was where I belonged.

Enterprise

Operating at Scale at NOOK (Barnes & Noble)

Working inside a billion-dollar organization taught me that scale doesn’t eliminate complexity—it amplifies it. The same organizational challenges that show up in startups also exist in large enterprises; they just have more zeros attached. At NOOK, every product change touched millions of readers and carried meaningful financial impact, which meant rigor mattered: instrumentation, testing, phased rollouts, and deep cross-functional coordination.

I also learned how powerful it is to understand the full ecosystem end-to-end. By diving into how ecommerce, digital reading, and physical retail fit together, I was able to identify opportunities others couldn’t see. That holistic perspective led to some of the most impactful work I did at Barnes & Noble—work that succeeded because it bridged disciplines that rarely talked to one another.

High-Growth

Leading Through Velocity at Simple & ChowNow

Leading Product in hyper-growth is a mix of rocket fuel and turbulence. Signals come fast, noise comes faster, and inexperienced teams often react to everything as if it’s brand-new. I learned early that staying grounded in your goals, your strategy, and your prior decisions is the only way to keep an organization healthy during rapid acceleration. Before revisiting a choice, I’d ask: Has truly new information come to light? If not, we move forward with confidence.

Hyper-growth also revealed character—both individual and organizational. It’s easy to feel aligned when everything is going well. The real measure of a team is how it behaves under pressure: whether people support one another, stay focused on shared outcomes, and act with clarity instead of panic. Those years taught me how to build resilience, not just velocity.

Product Leadership & Advisory

Bringing Clarity to Early-Stage Teams

Advising founders and early-stage teams has reinforced something I’ve seen throughout my career: most organizations don’t struggle because they lack talent—they struggle because they lack clarity. My work often starts with helping teams focus on one thing at a time, prioritize deliberately, and understand why a single coordinated effort outperforms scattered activity every time.

I teach teams how to break overwhelming initiatives into clear, actionable pieces and how to communicate priorities so everyone rows in the same direction. It’s remarkable how quickly momentum builds once a team understands what matters, why it matters, and what “good” looks like.

One of the strongest signals that an organization needs product leadership maturity is when people are unsure what product management is for. Those moments are an invitation—an opportunity to introduce structure, alignment, and shared understanding so the whole organization can operate with far more confidence and coherence.
