3 Roles That Shaped My Approach

Systems thinking, human-centered leadership, and joyful delivery.

2004 - 2007

Right Media — Instincts & Craft

  • Scrappy, end-to-end product work across research → UX → QA → launch
  • Introduced tri-state checkbox; user-loved, later adopted widely
  • Foundation: trust instincts; validate with users; invent when needed
Details

Early in my career, I joined Right Media — a fast-paced startup building the first exchange for online display advertising — as a solutions architect (which, in practice, meant scrappy, end-to-end product management). I was surrounded by brilliant people doing wild, ambitious work, and I didn't understand half of what was going on for the first few months — which only made me more determined to figure it out.

With no real guardrails, I taught myself everything from user research and interaction design to writing specs, test cases, and QA plans. I even introduced a new UI concept — the tri-state checkbox — that engineers initially pushed back on, but which users loved and later became standard in native iOS apps.

That product remained beloved and heavily used long after our acquisition by Yahoo!, and it gave me a lasting foundation: trust your instincts, back them up with real user insight, and don't be afraid to invent the thing that doesn't exist yet.

2007 - 2012

Barnes & Noble — Scale & Systems

  • Led Search & Personalization; promoted to Director; cross-org initiatives
  • Restored clarity and momentum with a shared vision and roadmap
  • Built data/CMS systems; filed a patent; drove org transformation
Details

After years in startups, I joined Barnes & Noble to lead Search & Personalization for bn.com — drawn by the challenge of shaping a technical, high-impact product in a legacy retail environment. I was quickly promoted to Director of Product Management, overseeing a team of PMs and leading strategic initiatives across e-commerce, data systems, and content.

When my VP left, I stepped up to lead the org through a moment of deep uncertainty: morale was low, teams were disconnected, and no one knew what we were building. I created clarity fast — developing a shared vision and roadmap, getting cross-functional buy-in (including from retail, which had never been brought into .com planning before), and restoring momentum.

Alongside that, I became the "go-to" for impossible projects — designing data systems, CMS infrastructure, and even filing a patent for a new book discovery engine. It was my first taste of organizational transformation work, and the moment I realized I could not only build products, but cultures, too.

2012 - 2020

ChowNow — Org Builder & Joyful Delivery

  • Led Product, Design, Engineering, Analytics; restructured the org
  • Enabled 10 parallel teams; Order Better Network ~30% revenue
  • Fastest, most bug-free Google pilot; systems and shared goals over "tension"
Details

As Chief Product Officer at ChowNow, I led Product, Design, Engineering, and Analytics through a period of rapid growth and evolution. When I joined, the org was underpowered and tangled. Misaligned roles and a fractured team culture led to multiple departures in my first few months.

I provided clarity, cohesion, and momentum by restructuring the org, hiring exceptional leaders across disciplines, and enabling parallel product development to replace the existing bottlenecked model. We scaled to 10 concurrent teams and launched major new initiatives, including the Order Better Network, which ultimately drove 30% of company revenue.

Under my leadership, we also became the fastest and most bug-free integration partner in a pilot with Google — a milestone their team called the best they'd seen. More than anything, this role clarified one of my core beliefs: high-performing teams don't need "healthy tension" between disciplines — they need shared goals, mutual respect, and leaders who know how to build both products and the systems that bring them to life.
