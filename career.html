Career Journey

From Scrappy Startup PM to Chief Product Officer

My 20+ year career has been a journey of building products, leading teams, and creating the conditions for impossible things to take shape. Here's how I went from learning on the fly at a fast-paced startup to leading product strategy at scale.

My Career Story

How I became the product leader I am today

I'm a product and operations leader with over 20 years of experience solving complex, cross-functional problems — the kind that require deep listening, thoughtful planning, and a team of smart people pulling in the same direction. My work lives at the intersection of systems thinking and human-centered leadership: part creative problem solving, part organizational design.

I specialize in understanding the full landscape — business goals, customer needs, and technical constraints — and crafting a product vision that optimizes for all three. I design the conditions for products to get built by setting direction, testing assumptions, prioritizing outcomes, and ensuring everyone is aligned and empowered to do their best work.

Colleagues describe me as easy to talk to and refreshingly direct. I lead with thoughtfulness, clarity, and a focus on execution — while always staying open to new ideas and ways of thinking. I value transparency, clear communication, and healthy collaboration. And I'm serious about building teams that ship great work.

Right Media

Solutions Architect / Product Manager

Early in my career, I joined this fast-paced startup building the first exchange for online display advertising. With no real guardrails, I taught myself everything from user research and interaction design to writing specs, test cases, and QA plans. I even introduced a new UI concept — the tri-state checkbox — that engineers initially pushed back on, but which users loved and later became standard in native iOS apps. That product remained beloved and heavily used long after our acquisition by Yahoo!, and it gave me a lasting foundation: trust your instincts, back them up with real user insight, and don't be afraid to invent the thing that doesn't exist yet.

Barnes & Noble

Director of Product Management

After years in startups, I joined Barnes & Noble to lead Search & Personalization for bn.com — drawn by the challenge of shaping a technical, high-impact product in a legacy retail environment. I was quickly promoted to Director of Product Management, overseeing a team of PMs and leading strategic initiatives across e-commerce, data systems, and content. When my VP left, I stepped up to lead the org through a moment of deep uncertainty: morale was low, teams were disconnected, and no one knew what we were building. I created clarity fast — developing a shared vision and roadmap, getting cross-functional buy-in (including from retail, which had never been brought into .com planning before), and restoring momentum. Alongside that, I became the "go-to" for impossible projects — designing data systems, CMS infrastructure, and even filing a patent for a new book discovery engine. It was my first taste of organizational transformation work, and the moment I realized I could not only build products, but cultures, too.

ChowNow

Chief Product Officer

As Chief Product Officer at ChowNow, I led Product, Design, Engineering, and Analytics through a period of rapid growth and evolution. When I joined, the org was underpowered and tangled. Misaligned roles and a fractured team culture led to multiple departures in my first few months. I provided clarity, cohesion, and momentum by restructuring the org, hiring exceptional leaders across disciplines, and enabling parallel product development to replace the existing bottlenecked model. We scaled to 10 concurrent teams and launched major new initiatives, including the Order Better Network, which ultimately drove 30% of company revenue. Under my leadership, we also became the fastest and most bug-free integration partner in a pilot with Google — a milestone their team called the best they'd seen. More than anything, this role clarified one of my core beliefs: high-performing teams don't need "healthy tension" between disciplines — they need shared goals, mutual respect, and leaders who know how to build both products and the systems that bring them to life.

Key Learnings

The principles that guide my leadership approach

🎯

Clarity Creates Momentum

When teams know what they're building and why, they can move fast and make smart decisions. I've learned that investing time upfront to create shared understanding pays massive dividends in execution speed and quality.

⚙️

Systems Over Heroics

Building sustainable, scalable processes is always better than relying on individual heroics. I focus on creating systems that enable teams to thrive and deliver consistently, even when I'm not in the room.

🤝

Culture Drives Performance

High-performing teams don't happen by accident. They're built through intentional culture work: shared goals, mutual respect, clear communication, and leaders who model the behavior they want to see.

🚀

Trust Your Instincts

Data and user research are crucial, but so is trusting your gut when something feels right. Some of my best product decisions came from following intuition backed by real user insight.
